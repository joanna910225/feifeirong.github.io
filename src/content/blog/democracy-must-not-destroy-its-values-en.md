> **The idea in one line:** A technology provider should not replace legitimate policy makers, but it still needs enforceable boundaries against uses that destroy the values the technology is supposed to defend.

## Policy decisions and provider boundaries are different

Asked how Anthropic thinks about supplying AI to the military, Dario Amodei separated two responsibilities.

The provider should not decide which military operation a government may conduct. Those choices belong to accountable military and political decision makers. A technology company cannot quietly turn itself into a parallel foreign-policy authority.

But supplying general capability does not require moral indifference. A provider can still set high-level boundaries that prohibit categories of use inconsistent with its values and support uses that are compatible with them.

That distinction avoids two bad extremes:

- attempting to judge every individual operation from inside a private company
- treating every possible use as acceptable once a contract has been signed

## Values need operational red lines

“Support democracy” is too vague to guide deployment on its own. In the interview, Dario attached the principle to concrete boundaries, including opposition to mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapons.

The deeper test is whether a method undermines the values it claims to protect. If a democratic society adopts the same coercive machinery it condemns in authoritarian systems, winning becomes self-defeating: the institution survives, but the reason for defending it has been traded away.

Useful principles therefore need three properties:

1. **They name the value being protected.**
2. **They identify prohibited classes of behavior.**
3. **They remain in force when following them becomes commercially or politically expensive.**

Without the third property, a principle is only branding.

## Means are part of the outcome

This is not only a military or AI-governance question. It applies whenever someone argues that a good end justifies a corrosive method.

The practical question is:

> If we succeed by using this method, will we still possess the value we claimed to be defending?

That connects to [Leave When Trust Collapses, Not When People Disagree](/feifeirong.github.io/blog/trust-collapse-not-disagreement-en/) and [Human-Centered Work Is the Point](/feifeirong.github.io/blog/human-centered-is-key-en/). Across all three, the layer that should not be optimized away is the human one: honesty, trust, dignity, and values that remain binding under pressure.
